**Problems** in the navigation lists everything in the activity, grouped by
series. Each row is `[slug] Name`, a status badge, and a **Submit** button.

The slug is the short label — `A`, `P1`, `knapsack` — and it is what appears in
the address. It is unique across the whole activity, not only within its round.

## Your standing on a problem

The badge on the right of each row is yours alone, not a global figure. It
carries up to three things:

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `12 / 100` | your **best score** that counts, against the maximum |
| `Attempts: 3` | how many submissions you have made |
| the badge itself | *Solved*, *Partial*, *Attempted* or *Not attempted* |

Best score is the score that counts under the activity's rules, so an
[excluded submission](/en/client/participant/results) is not in it. Where you
compete in a group, both figures are the group's.

The same badge sits at the top of the statement, because the statement is where
you sit while waiting for a verdict — and it moves there as verdicts arrive.

## Why Submit is greyed out

<Callout type="warn" title="A disabled button, not a missing one">
The button stays where it has always been and says it is shut rather than
vanishing. It is disabled whenever the round holding the problem is not
accepting: it has **ended**, a manager has **paused** it, or it has not started.
</Callout>

The statement screen also carries a badge saying which of the two it is — *The
series is paused* in orange, *The series has ended* in grey — so you can read the
reason where you are looking at the button.

The submit form itself repeats it in a sentence before you write anything. See
[Rounds, weeks and classes](/en/client/participant/series) for the full table.

## The statement

Opening a problem gives you its title as `[slug] Name`, your status badge, and
whatever the assignment states about limits and your remaining submissions.

### More than one language

Where the author wrote translations, a switcher appears above the statement:
**Default statement** plus one entry per language. It changes the statement
alone, not the interface — wanting the English statement in a Polish interface
is an ordinary thing to want.

You get the statement in your interface language where the author wrote one, and
what they wrote first otherwise. A missing translation falls back; it is never an
error.

Statements are normally Markdown. A problem imported from an archive may carry a
PDF instead, and it is drawn in a viewer on the page rather than handed to you as
a download link.

### Limits

Two badges beside the title — a time and a memory figure — are the assignment's
own pair, before anything narrows it.

Below the statement a **Limits** card may carry **two tables**, and they are two
rather than one on purpose:

- **Per test group** — the same for every language.
- **Per language** — the same for every group.

There is never a value on both at once, and a group's own limits win over a
language override rather than combining with it. Rows a language override does
not reach are marked with an asterisk. A single table would have to invent the
cell where the two cross.

**Nothing shown means the assignment overrode nothing** — not "no limit". A
package's own numbers are not published to participants, so the screen says
nothing rather than guessing.

### Attachments

An **Attachments** card lists every file scoped to you, with its size in kB. The
statement's own files are not in it; everything else the author attached is.

Where the problem carries sample tests, a line under the statement says they are
also available as separate files.

### Submissions left

Where the activity sets an allowance, a **Submissions left** badge says how many.
At three or fewer the submit form raises it to a warning. Absent means unlimited.

In a group the allowance is the group's, spent by whoever sends.
